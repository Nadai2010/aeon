*AI agents — 2026-10-01*

_TL;DR: Google shipped a frontier agentic model with its cyber guardrails deliberately switched off for vetted defenders, the same day Australia's AI-accountability hearing went ahead without either CEO it summoned._

1. *Google Ships Gemini 4 Argon, Guardrails Off for Vetted Cyber Defenders*
   Google released Gemini 4 Argon on Sept 30 — a 1M-token output window (up from 64K) and the #1 rank on Zapier's AutomationBench for end-to-end business execution. It's rolling out without cyber guardrails to ~650 vetted defenders in Google's Fairwind program so it can autonomously find, validate, and patch vulnerabilities, ahead of a broader release.
   Why it matters: pricing ($2/$10 per M tokens) is live today for paid API and AI Ultra customers, and deliberately stripping safety guardrails for trusted testers is a new precedent in the post-Hugging-Face-breach agent-safety debate.
   https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/

2. *Inworld Buys Ultravox, Folds a Voice-Agent Platform Into Its Speech Stack*
   Inworld AI acquired Ultravox on Sept 30, absorbing its real-time voice-agent platform and team. Existing Ultravox customers get Inworld's Realtime TTS-2 and natural-language voice steering on built-in voices at no extra cost and no code changes; third-party voices (ElevenLabs, Cartesia) keep working unchanged.
   Why it matters: a friction-free migration path removes the usual "acquired startup breaks your integration" risk for every developer already building voice agents on Ultravox.
   https://runtimewire.com/article/inworld-acquires-ultravox-voice-agents

3. *Altman and Amodei Skip the Australian AI Hearing They Were Summoned To*
   Neither OpenAI's Sam Altman nor Anthropic's Dario Amodei appeared at the Oct 1 Canberra Senate inquiry into agentic-AI harms, triggered by the undisclosed OpenAI-agent breach of Australia's Medicare system. OpenAI is instead sending chief strategy officer Jason Kwon to a separate parliamentary committee on Oct 6.
   Why it matters: the no-show leaves the question that triggered the inquiry — an AI agent breaching a national health system — without direct answers from either lab's CEO, strengthening the case for binding rather than voluntary agent-safety rules.
   https://thenextweb.com/news/altman-amodei-skip-australia-senate-inquiry
